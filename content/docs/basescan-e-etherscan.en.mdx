title: Where is my money?
description: Understand the link support sent you.

Cryptocurrencies are sent and received over networks. Base, Ethereum and Solana are different networks. Your asset balances and movements are recorded on those networks, associated with wallet addresses.

Each network has an explorer that works like a **public statement**. A wallet address lets you look up its history on that network; a hash (the transaction ID) lets you look up a specific transaction.

**Important:** unfamiliar tokens and transactions may be spam or scam attempts. Do not open promotional links associated with them, connect your wallet or sign approvals to claim them. Simply appearing in the statement does not give them access to your wallet.

<Callout title="Just want to check your money?">
  Start with your **balance and history in Conta**. If you received a BaseScan or
  Etherscan link, it lets you check the network record: **what moved, to which
  address, and the transaction's status**. Your money is not held on these
  websites; they display the records.
</Callout>

Public statements for these networks are available at:

| Website | Network |
| --- | --- |
| [basescan.org](https://basescan.org) | Base |
| [etherscan.io](https://etherscan.io) | Ethereum |
| [solscan.io](https://solscan.io) | Solana |

Each network has its own records. A transaction on Base should be checked in a Base explorer (“public statement”). Not finding it on Ethereum does not mean the money disappeared: check that you searched the correct network.

**You do not need to create an account or connect your wallet to look up records.**

## How to read the public statement

The link opens an explorer (“public statement”) for one of the networks.

We use a Base wallet as the example here. Other explorers have different layouts but offer similar information.

### A–C: wallet overview

![BaseScan: A, B, C.](/images/guides/basescan-wallet-abc.png)

| Label | Where to look | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Address at the top and in the URL | Identifies the wallet whose statement is open. |
| **B** | Token Holdings | Estimated dollar value of tokens the explorer can price. It may not include every asset. |
| **C** | Token Transfers (ERC-20) | Tab showing token movements on Base. |

### D–G: token transfers

![BaseScan: D, E, F, G.](/images/guides/basescan-transfers-defg.png)

| Label | Where to look | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **D** | Transaction Hash | Transaction ID. Select it to open the details. |
| **E** | Age / time | Select or hover to check the date and time. Check the displayed time zone: when it is UTC, Brasília is three hours behind (UTC−3); other Brazilian regions use different time zones. |
| **F** | From / To and OUT / IN | From is the source; To is the destination. OUT means outgoing and IN incoming relative to the wallet being viewed. |
| **G** | Value (USD) / Token | Estimated dollar value and the asset moved. Check the token quantity too; the dollar estimate is not a balance in reais or a redemption guarantee. |

<Callout title="For Pix payments">
  A confirmed token transfer lets you check the movement between the recorded
  addresses. Check the asset, amount and destination in the details:
  **“Success” alone does not prove a Pix payment reached its recipient**.
  If the operation involves Pix, also follow the payment status in your Conta
  history.
</Callout>

## Questions about the page

<Accordions type="single">
  <Accordion title="It says 0 ETH. Has my money disappeared?" value="zero-eth" id="zero-eth">
    The **Value** field in ETH shows how much ETH was sent in that transaction.
    A transfer of another asset, such as cBRL or USDC, can show **0 ETH** while
    still moving that asset. Check **Tokens Transferred** to see what moved.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why does it show cBRL instead of reais?" value="cbrl" id="cbrl">
    cBRL is the digital asset behind the balance shown in reais in Conta. Its
    model aims to represent R$1 per unit. Read more about
    [your balance and stablecoins](/en/docs/dinheiro).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I put money in my Reserve. Where does it appear?" value="reserva" id="reserva">
    Your Reserve has its own address, linked to your wallet. Money you set aside
    can appear at that address rather than in your wallet's available balance.
    See [how your Reserve works](/en/docs/vaults).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The To address is not the person's address. Why?" value="destino" id="destino">
    **To** or **Interacted With (To)** may identify a contract used in the
    operation. For a token transfer, check the recipient under
    **Tokens Transferred**. If you do not recognise the destination, ask support
    for help.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is Transaction Fee? Do I need to pay to look this up?" value="taxa" id="taxa">
    **Transaction Fee** is the cost of processing the transaction on the network.
    It can apply even when an attempt fails. This field is not a payment request
    to you. **Looking up the page is free** and does not require sending money
    or approving a transaction.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I cannot find the transaction or the balance looks wrong. What now?" value="suporte" id="suporte">
    Check that you are looking at the correct network. If you still need help,
    send Conta support the **link or Transaction Hash**, the **expected amount**
    and the **approximate time**. The hash is the code that identifies the
    transaction. For a Pix payment, include the receipt. Check what happened
    before repeating the payment.
  </Accordion>
</Accordions>

**You do not need to share passwords, passkeys or private keys to check a
record.** The link can reveal amounts, addresses and public transaction history;
share these details with care.
