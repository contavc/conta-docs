You've seen where your assets are held. Now imagine opening Conta and
seeing a balance of R$100. How does a digital asset become a balance
expressed in reais?

The connection starts with **stablecoins**: digital assets designed to
track a currency's value. A real-denominated stablecoin aims for R$1
per unit. A dollar stablecoin uses the US dollar as its reference.

## A familiar unit for everyday money

A balance linked to the real lets you think about money in the same unit
as your everyday expenses. A dollar asset has a different reference:
its value in reais changes with the exchange rate.

A stablecoin's value depends on what supports it and how it can be exchanged
back. That support is called **backing**. The organisation responsible for
its issuance and redemption is its **issuer**.

## Where cBRL enters the story

In Conta, **cBRL** represents your balance backed by real-denominated
stablecoins. Its 1:1 model aims for 100 cBRL to represent R$100.

The app shows an amount in reais, while cBRL is the digital asset behind
that representation. Fees and conversion rates can affect how much arrives
in a particular operation.

Why add cBRL instead of depending on one stablecoin? That choice is about
having more than one provider to work with.
