---
title: Why cBRL?
description: A balance in reais that lets Conta work with different stablecoin providers.
---

You've seen how a stablecoin connects digital assets to a familiar currency.
**cBRL**, or contaBRL, is the token used for your balance in reais at Conta.
In the current Base deployment, **BRLA** is the configured backing asset. The
model can also integrate other compatible real-denominated stablecoins, such
as BRS; this does not mean both back the current deployment.

A stablecoin is a digital asset designed to track a currency. Here, the
reference is the Brazilian real: the aim is to keep **1 unit equivalent
to R$1**.

## Reduce reliance on a single provider

Using only one stablecoin can concentrate operations with a single provider.
If it becomes unavailable, that dependence can interrupt the path used to
receive or send money.

cBRL separates the balance presented to the user from the integration with
a specific stablecoin. This can support compatible alternatives while keeping one cBRL balance
experience, once those alternatives are actually available.

## Another path when a provider is unavailable

If another compatible provider is integrated and available, Conta can direct
new operations through it. That would not automatically recover a payment
already in progress or release backing held by an unavailable provider. The user keeps the same balance reference without
having to choose a new token for each payment path.

This is the idea of **redundancy**: having an alternative to reduce dependence
on a single service. It depends on the alternative provider's availability
and capacity to handle the operation.

## What does wrapping a stablecoin mean?

cBRL represents another asset. This model is also called *wrapping*: the
backing stablecoin is held in reserve, while the user receives the
corresponding value in cBRL in their wallet.

For example, under the 1:1 model, 100 units of a real-denominated stablecoin
back 100 cBRL, before any fees. On redemption, the corresponding cBRL leaves
circulation and the backing is released according to the operation's rules.

This lets Conta present a balance in reais without requiring the user to
handle each stablecoin integration separately.

## What the peg means

The 1:1 reference guides issuance and redemption. An operation's net amount
may include fees, and the ability to redeem depends on reserves, issuers
and the system's operating conditions.

cBRL preserves this connection to its backing asset; it also carries that
asset's risks. Assessing its security therefore includes both cBRL's contracts
and the reserves and issuers of the stablecoins supporting it.
