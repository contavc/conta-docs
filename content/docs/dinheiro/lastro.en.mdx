The previous page introduced cBRL as a way to work with different providers.
Now let's meet two real-denominated stablecoins in that model: BRLA and BRS.

cBRL's backing has layers. Knowing each layer's role helps you understand
which information to check and who is responsible for publishing it.

| Layer | Role |
| --- | --- |
| cBRL in the wallet | Represents the user's digital balance. |
| Backing stablecoin | Supports the corresponding issuance of cBRL. |
| Issuer reserves | Support the value of the stablecoin used as backing. |

## BRLA

**Avenia** describes BRLA as a fully backed stablecoin, pegged 1:1 to the real,
with reserve disclosures. It reports reserves held in Brazilian government
bonds and regulated custody.
See [BRLA's official information](https://avenia.io/brla).

## BRS

**Nora** describes BRS as a fully reserved stablecoin with a 1:1 reference to
the real, with issuance and redemption through participants in its network.
See [BRS's official information](https://www.nora.finance/).

## How to interpret this information

BRLA is the configured backing asset in the current Base deployment of cBRL.
BRS illustrates another real-denominated stablecoin compatible with the model;
its description here does not mean that it already backs cBRL in this deployment.
The asset actually used matters when assessing an issuance.

Network records help verify tokens and contract movements, but cannot by
themselves prove financial reserves held off-chain. For that layer, consult
the information and attestations published by the issuer. cBRL preserves the connection to the asset used and
also depends on its redemption conditions.
