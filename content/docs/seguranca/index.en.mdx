Before talking about balances, start with the wallet. A **digital wallet**
lets you hold and use digital assets. It has an address, which identifies
it on the network, and a way to authorise transactions.

Conta uses a **non-custodial wallet**. The idea is that authorising the use
of your wallet's assets is tied to your access and approval. This is the
foundation of self-custody: taking an active role in controlling your money.

## What belongs to the wallet?

Imagine someone sends you a digital asset. The network records it at your
wallet address. Conta gives you a familiar way to view that balance and
authorise an operation, such as a transfer.

The wallet's key is what makes that authorisation possible. You don't need
to handle a long code for every payment: the app brings that authorisation
into your sign-in and confirmation experience.

## Control comes with responsibility

Keeping access to your wallet secure matters. Protecting your sign-in
method and checking what you authorise are part of looking after your
money. Practical access guidance belongs in the
[help centre](https://www.conta.vc/ajuda).

In the app, you can manage the **passkeys** you use to sign in, including adding
another one or removing one you no longer use. Review your access methods
before removing the last device available to you.

An address can be used to look up public activity. Knowing an address
doesn't give someone the key that authorises its transactions.

Your authority to sign and the token's rules are separate layers. The cBRL
contract has administrative controls, including pauses, freezes and compliance
with legal orders. Wallet self-custody does not remove those rules or the
backing stablecoin issuer's risks.

## A wallet holds assets, but what gives them value?

The wallet explains where an asset is recorded and how you authorise its
use. It doesn't, on its own, explain what that asset is worth.
